## 1. Objective vs. Search: Decomposing What Makes a Good Tokeniser

**Authors:** Ahmetcan Yavuz, Clara Meister, Tiago Pimentel

**TL;DR:** The study shows that the search strategy—bottom‑up versus top‑down—has a larger impact on tokenisation compression than whether the algorithm optimises for compression or likelihood.

**Key Innovation:** Introducing two new tokenisers that disentangle optimisation objective from search procedure, completing the 2×2 design space of bottom‑up/top‑down and compression/likelihood, and revealing that search dominates compression performance.

**Why It Matters:** Engineers building multilingual language‑model pipelines can adopt bottom‑up tokenisers to achieve lower bits‑per‑byte and smaller vocabularies, reducing storage costs and speeding up inference in production systems.

**Tags:** tokenisation, compression, search-procedure, language-modeling, multilingual

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19145v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19145v1)

## 2. A Zeroth-Order Paradigm for LLM Preference Alignment

**Authors:** Peter Chen, Xi Chen, Wotao Yin, Tianyi Lin

**TL;DR:** ComPO aligns large language models by using comparison oracles to extract directional preference signals without gradient-based loss, improving alignment quality and reducing likelihood displacement.

**Key Innovation:** A zeroth-order preference optimization framework (ComPO) that leverages comparison oracles, with provable convergence for offline and online settings and reverse‑KL control for policy regularization.

**Why It Matters:** It enables more efficient and memory‑friendly alignment of commercial LLMs for chat or content‑moderation systems, yielding better consistency and less length bias in real‑world deployments.

**Tags:** zeroth-order, preference-alignment, comparison-oracle, offline-learning, online-learning

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19144v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19144v1)

## 3. Dreaming the Sound of Contact: Leveraging Video and Audio Generation for Zero-Shot Force-Aware Manipulation and Data Generation

**Authors:** Guanhua Ji, Tianyu Li, Dayoon Suh, Yuqian Zhang, Boyan Zhang, Nadia Figueroa

**TL;DR:** The paper demonstrates how AI-generated videos and their accompanying contact sounds can be used to produce realistic force profiles, enabling a robot to perform contact-rich tasks without prior force data.

**Key Innovation:** A joint video‑audio generation pipeline that derives motion trajectories and a bounded, time‑varying desired‑force profile from a natural‑language prompt, using audio loudness to shape force control.

**Why It Matters:** It allows engineers to deploy robots that can handle delicate, contact‑rich operations—such as assembling small parts or performing quality inspections—without expensive force sensors or hand‑crafted force models.

**Tags:** video-generation, audio-generation, force-aware-manipulation, zero-shot-learning, closed-loop-control

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19137v1)
