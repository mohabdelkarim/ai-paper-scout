## 1. Rethinking the Divergence Regularization in LLM RL

**Authors:** Jiarui Yao, Xiangxin Zhou, Penghui Qi, Wee Sun Lee, Liefeng Bo, Tianyu Pang

**TL;DR:** This paper proposes Divergence Regularized Policy Optimization, a new method for stabilizing reinforcement learning in large language models by replacing hard masks with smooth regularizers

**Key Innovation:** Replacing the hard mask in DPPO with a smooth advantage-weighted quadratic regularizer to induce bounded, continuous gradient weights

**Why It Matters:** This innovation improves the stability and efficiency of large language model reinforcement learning training, allowing for more effective and reliable optimization

**Tags:** Reinforcement Learning, Large Language Models, Trust Region Optimization, Policy Optimization, Natural Language Processing

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09821v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09821v1)

## 2. iOSWorld: A Benchmark for Personally Intelligent Phone Agents

**Authors:** Lawrence Keunho Jang, Mareks Woodside, Geronimo Carom, Andrew Keunwoo Jang, Jing Yu Koh, Ruslan Salakhutdinov

**TL;DR:** This paper introduces iOSWorld, a benchmark for evaluating personally intelligent phone agents that can reason over a user's identity, history, and preferences across multiple iOS apps

**Key Innovation:** The creation of a native iOS simulator benchmark with a persistent user identity and connected data across 26 apps, enabling the evaluation of agents' personalization and multi-app reasoning capabilities

**Why It Matters:** The development of personally intelligent phone agents has significant practical implications for improving user experience, enhancing mobile device functionality, and enabling more effective human-computer interaction

**Tags:** Artificial Intelligence, Mobile Agents, Personalization, Benchmarking, Human-Computer Interaction

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09764v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09764v1)

## 3. Collaborative Human-Agent Protocol (CHAP)

**Authors:** Arsalan Shahid, Gordon Suttie, Philip Black

**TL;DR:** The Collaborative Human-Agent Protocol (CHAP) is a standardized protocol for human-agent collaboration, enabling accountable and transparent work across teams, time zones, and trust boundaries

**Key Innovation:** The introduction of a structured event-based protocol for human-agent interaction, allowing for non-repudiable signed decisions, portable envelopes, and transparent audit trails

**Why It Matters:** CHAP has significant implications for the deployment of foundation models in operational roles, enabling more efficient, accountable, and transparent human-agent collaboration, and improving the reliability and trustworthiness of AI-driven decision-making

**Tags:** human-agent collaboration, foundation models, protocol standards, accountability, transparency

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09751v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09751v1)
