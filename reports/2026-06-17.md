## 1. Visual Verification Enables Inference-time Steering and Autonomous Policy Improvement

**Authors:** Mingtong Zhang, Dhruv Shah

**TL;DR:** This paper proposes a framework called VERITAS that enables robots to learn from their experiences and improve their policies in real-time using visual verification and inference-time steering

**Key Innovation:** The use of a gradient-free visual verifier to evaluate actions at inference time, allowing for autonomous policy improvement without additional training

**Why It Matters:** This approach has practical implications for improving the performance and efficiency of robotic policies during deployment, reducing the need for human interventions and expert demonstrations

**Tags:** robotics, reinforcement_learning, autonomous_systems, visual_verification, inference_time_steering

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18247v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18247v1)

## 2. Looped World Models

**Authors:** Hongyuan Adam Lu, Z. L. Victor Wei, Qun Zhang, Jinrui Zeng, Bowen Cao, Lingwei Meng, Mocheng Li, Zezhong Wang, Haonan Yin, Naifu Xue, Minyu Chen, Cenyuan Zhang, Zefan Zhang, Hao Wei, Jiawei Zhou, Haoran Xu, Hao Yang, Ronglai Zuo, Tongda Xu, Yonghao Li, Jian Chen, Hebin Wang, Zeyu Gao, Yang Li, Wei Zhao, Qimin Zhong, Siqi Liu, Yumeng Zhang, Leyan Cui, Zhangyu Wang, Wai Lam

**TL;DR:** This paper introduces Looped World Models, a novel architecture that iteratively refines environment states using a shared transformer block, achieving up to 100x parameter efficiency over conventional approaches

**Key Innovation:** The use of a parameter-shared transformer block to create a looped architecture for world modeling, allowing for adaptive computation and iterative latent depth

**Why It Matters:** This innovation enables more efficient and scalable world modeling, which can significantly impact applications such as simulation, planning, and decision-making in complex environments

**Tags:** world models, transformer architectures, efficient computation, scalable simulation, artificial intelligence

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18208v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18208v1)

## 3. Knowledge Reutilization in Meta-Reinforcement Learning

**Authors:** Yuan Meng, Bo Wang, Juan de los Rios Ruiz, Xiangtong Yao, Zhenshan Bing, Fuchun Sun, Alois Knoll

**TL;DR:** This paper proposes a meta-knowledge reutilization framework for meta-reinforcement learning, which enables the transfer of task-level knowledge across different agents and improves sample efficiency

**Key Innovation:** The use of a Bayesian non-parametric prior to organize latent task modes and a semantic-magnitude interface to bridge reusable task knowledge with different embodiments

**Why It Matters:** The framework achieves significant improvements in tracking error and deployment performance while reducing the required interaction data, making it a promising approach for real-world applications of meta-reinforcement learning

**Tags:** meta-reinforcement learning, knowledge transfer, Bayesian non-parametric methods, task semantics, embodiment-specific control

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18132v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18132v1)
