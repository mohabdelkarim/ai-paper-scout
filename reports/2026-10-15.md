## 1. Modeling and Understanding Neural Weights of Humanoid Policies with Diffusion

**Authors:** Huixuan Cheng, Bike Zhang, Shankar Sastry, Koushil Sreenath

**TL;DR:** We can learn a generative model of humanoid control networks directly from their weights, enabling new networks that perform real‑world motions.

**Key Innovation:** A latent diffusion model that compresses actor network parameters via a linear autoencoder and uses a Diffusion Transformer to learn the weight distribution, allowing generation of functional humanoid policies.

**Why It Matters:** Engineers can quickly synthesize new high‑performance humanoid controllers without costly reinforcement learning, speeding deployment of diverse motions like walking or dancing on real robots.

**Tags:** latent-diffusion, humanoid-robotics, policy-generation, weight-space-modeling, reinforcement-learning

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20606v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20606v1)

## 2. Hierarchical Residual Policy Learning for Real-World Mobile Manipulation with Sparse Human Guidance

**Authors:** Zhefei Gong, Hefeifei Jiang, Zehao Wang, Jiachen Li

**TL;DR:** HiRe-MoMa introduces a hierarchical residual framework that fine‑tunes frozen mobile‑manipulation policies with sparse human guidance, achieving sample‑efficient real‑world performance in under 30 minutes.

**Key Innovation:** A policy‑agnostic residual network split into mobile‑base and manipulator‑arm heads with unidirectional conditioning, combined with expert‑guided value alignment to reshape the critic using sparse interventions.

**Why It Matters:** Enables rapid, low‑data deployment of complex mobile‑manipulation robots (e.g., warehouse pickers or domestic assistants) in dynamic, human‑centric settings without extensive retraining.

**Tags:** hierarchical-residual-architecture, mobile-manipulation, real-world-fine-tuning, sparse-human-guidance, value-alignment

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20607v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20607v1)

## 3. Co-Evolving Acting and Forecasting for Language Agents

**Authors:** Haofei Yu, Yixing Chen, Yining Zhao, Xuyan Ye, Yuxiang Liu, Zhigang Hua, Yan Xie, Shuang Yang, Jiaxuan You

**TL;DR:** CoPE trains a single language-agent policy to both act and forecast future actions by supervising multi-step action sequences, boosting sample efficiency without extra inference cost.

**Key Innovation:** A co-evolving framework that uses multi-step action targets to jointly train acting and forecasting within one policy backbone, enabling temporal ensemble learning from on-policy trajectories.

**Why It Matters:** It dramatically reduces the data and compute needed to train web‑navigation and e‑commerce agents, making it practical to deploy reliable language assistants for online shopping, customer support, and scientific data exploration.

**Tags:** reinforcement-learning, language-models, action-forecasting, sample-efficiency, auxiliary-supervision

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20597v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20597v1)
