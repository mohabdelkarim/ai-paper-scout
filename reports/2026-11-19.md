## 1. Cap-and-Track: Optimizing LLM Inference Request Dispatch Across Shifting Capacity

**Authors:** Benjamin Novotny, Doug Szeto, Chang Wang, Shicheng Chen, George Zhihua Yuan, Bob Kamma, Michael Ge, John Xin Zhou, Andrea Olgiati

**TL;DR:** Cap-and-Track is a dynamic request dispatch system for LLM inference that uses backend refusal signals to adjust traffic flow in real-time, eliminating the need for static rate limits or output length predictions.

**Key Innovation:** A stateful 'Track' dispatcher that probes and backs off based solely on backend admission refusals (fail-fast checks), allowing it to dynamically follow the current capacity ceiling without requiring fleet-level rate retuning or predicting generative output lengths.

**Why It Matters:** Enables production systems like Meta's live-video understanding to serve high-volume inference workloads with significantly higher hardware efficiency (43% fewer H100 GPUs) by dynamically adapting to shifting capacity and request sizes in real-time, reducing infrastructure costs while maintaining high throughput.

**Tags:** llm-inference, load-balancing, resource-management, systems-design, traffic-shaping

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24891v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24891v1)

## 2. Vibe-Trading: Towards Agent-Native Trading

**Authors:** Haozhe Wu, Zhonghang Li, Chao Huang

**TL;DR:** Vibe-Trading is a unified framework that enables AI agents to manage the full lifecycle of financial trading, from research to execution, while maintaining strict traceability and state consistency in dynamic market environments.

**Key Innovation:** An agent-native architecture that links every trading result to its specific data snapshot, temporal scope, and execution context, featuring a shared evidence grounding system and an explicit authority gate to separate research from live execution.

**Why It Matters:** Enables financial institutions and quantitative developers to deploy autonomous trading agents that are auditable and reproducible, reducing the risk of executing strategies based on stale data or unverified logic by enforcing a clear boundary between experimental research and live capital deployment.

**Tags:** ai-agents, financial-trading, agent-architecture, reproducibility, state-management

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24845v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24845v1)

## 3. Depth Before Breadth: Reverse-to-Forward Curriculum with Advantage Decomposition for LLM Agent

**Authors:** Siyuan Du, Yuxin Liu, Meng Tang, Haoran Li, Yechao Bai, Feiyang Pan, Jia He

**TL;DR:** This paper introduces a training method for AI agents that breaks down complex, long-term tasks into smaller, manageable steps by focusing on reaching intermediate 'solvable' states before completing the final goal, significantly improving performance on tasks like web navigation and household management.

**Key Innovation:** The introduction of a 'reverse-to-forward curriculum' combined with advantage decomposition, which separates the learning signal into two distinct parts: one for reaching a state where the task is solvable and another for completing the task from that state, thereby solving the credit assignment problem in long-horizon reinforcement learning.

**Why It Matters:** Enables the development of more reliable autonomous AI assistants capable of executing complex, multi-step workflows (such as booking travel or managing smart home devices) without failing due to the difficulty of learning from rare, final success signals, making these agents more practical for real-world deployment.

**Tags:** reinforcement-learning, llm-agents, curriculum-learning, credit-assignment, long-horizon-tasks

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24697v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24697v1)
