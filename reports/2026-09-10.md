## 1. Likelihood-free inference with nuisance parameters through normalizing flows

**Authors:** Phil Assheton

**TL;DR:** The paper shows how to use normalizing flows to automatically find near‑pivotal test statistics for problems with nuisance parameters, enabling fast and accurate likelihood‑free inference from simulated data.

**Key Innovation:** A decomposition of neural‑network normalizing flows that produces near‑pivotal statistics without needing a likelihood, even in the presence of nuisance parameters.

**Why It Matters:** It allows scientists and engineers to perform hypothesis tests and parameter estimation in complex simulation‑based experiments—such as particle‑physics analyses—more efficiently and with better statistical properties than traditional profile‑likelihood or Welch tests.

## 3. Characterizing Language Generation in the Limit: Finite Witnesses and a Separation-Width Hierarch

**Authors:** Xiaoyu Li, Andi Han, Jiaojiao Jiang, Junbin Gao

**TL;DR:** The paper shows that a language can be generated from an exhaustive list of positive examples exactly when every element has a finite witness that guarantees all finite samples share infinitely many common elements.

**Key Innovation:** A complete characterization of language generation in the limit via finite positive witnesses and the introduction of the positive separation width hierarchy, proven formally in Lean.

**Why It Matters:** It tells engineers when a generative model trained only on positive data can reliably produce new valid sentences, guiding the design of incremental learning systems and curriculum strategies in NLP.

**Tags:** language-learning, generation-in-the-limit, finite-witness, positive-separation-width, lean-verification

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10525v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10525v1)
