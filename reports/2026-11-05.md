## 1. A Framework for Context Enrichment and Selective Robustness in LLM Social Agents

**Authors:** Mattia Greiche, Reihaneh Rabbany, Maximilian Puelma Touzel

**TL;DR:** A framework that systematically enriches LLM prompts with neutral, social, descriptive, and normative context while introducing a selective robustness metric to assess agents’ stability to meaning‑preserving prompt changes and responsiveness to real‑world variations.

**Key Innovation:** The design of controlled context‑enrichment prompt substitution rules combined with a selective‑robustness validation criterion for LLM social agents.

**Why It Matters:** It enables engineers to build more reliable social‑simulation agents whose behavior remains consistent across minor prompt tweaks yet still reacts appropriately to policy or environment changes, improving the trustworthiness of AI‑driven policy analysis tools.

**Tags:** prompt-engineering, context-enrichment, selective-robustness, social-simulation, large-language-models

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06914v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06914v1)

## 2. FlowReconBench: Accuracy, calibration and cost of learned and classical flow reconstruction from sparse sensors

**Authors:** Nicolas Tricard, Linzheng Wang, Jason Chen, Sili Deng

**TL;DR:** FlowReconBench evaluates ten data‑driven and five classical flow‑reconstruction methods across laminar, turbulent, and 3D regimes, showing classical estimators excel with dense sensors while learned models outperform when sensors are sparse or unobservable, and that generative models can trade ensemble spread for accuracy via computational budget.

**Key Innovation:** A comprehensive, leakage‑controlled benchmark framework that systematically scores learned and classical flow‑reconstruction methods on error, calibration, spectra, and cost across multiple flow regimes.

**Why It Matters:** The benchmark guides engineers in selecting the most reliable reconstruction strategy for sensor‑limited flow monitoring and control in aerodynamics, weather prediction, and industrial process optimization.

**Tags:** flow-reconstruction, benchmark, data-driven, classical-estimators, generative-models

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06912v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06912v1)

## 3. BEAKER: An Expert-Curated Benchmark for Embodied Brains in Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories

**Authors:** Fei Lin, Tengchao Zhang, Ziyang Gong, Xiaotong Yu, Bohan Zhang, Yifan Zhou, Qihao Yang, Ji Dai, Dong Li, Yining Jiang, Qinghua Ni, Jun Huang, Qiang Zhang, Yue Zhou, Zhihang Zhong, Yonglin Tian, Fei-Yue Wang, Xue Yang

**TL;DR:** BEAKER is a 1,000‑sample, expert‑curated benchmark that tests multimodal large language models on the precise, action‑grounded perception and reasoning needed to autonomously run chemical experiments in self‑driving labs.

**Key Innovation:** The core contribution is a comprehensive, manually annotated benchmark covering 11 image and video task types—affordance grounding, trajectory planning, apparatus and state recognition, safety, setup, operation, step reasoning, and temporal procedure reasoning—specifically designed to evaluate embodied execution capabilities of MLLMs.

**Why It Matters:** By exposing the gap between coarse procedural understanding and fine‑grained, action‑ready perception, BEAKER enables engineers to build and validate autonomous laboratory systems that can reliably plan, execute, and monitor chemical syntheses, thereby accelerating drug discovery, materials research, and industrial chemistry workflows.

**Tags:** benchmark, self-driving-chemical-lab, embodied-ai, multimodal-language-models, chemical-experimentation

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06911v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06911v1)
