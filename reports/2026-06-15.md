## 1. ClinHallu: A Benchmark for Diagnosing Stage-Wise Hallucinations in Medical MLLM Reasoning

**Authors:** Sicheng Yang, Hangjie Yuan, Wenjun Zhang, Jinwang Wang, Yichen Qian, Weihua Chen, Fan Wang, Lei Zhu

**TL;DR:** ClinHallu is a benchmark for diagnosing stage-wise hallucinations in medical multimodal large language models, enabling fine-grained analysis of reasoning failures

**Key Innovation:** Decomposition of the reasoning process into Visual Recognition, Knowledge Recall, and Reasoning Integration stages with stage-replacement interventions

**Why It Matters:** ClinHallu provides a testbed for evaluating and mitigating hallucinations in medical language models, improving their reliability and trustworthiness for clinical decision support

**Tags:** Medical Multimodal Large Language Models, Hallucination Detection, Reasoning Process Analysis, Clinical Decision Support, Benchmarking

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14697v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14697v1)

## 2. CottonLeafVision: An Explainable and Robust Deep Learning Framework for Cotton Leaf Disease Classification

**Authors:** Rafi Ahamed, Md. Abir Rahman, Tasnia Tarannum Roza, Munaia Jannat Easha, Md. Asif Khan, Sudeepta Mandal

**TL;DR:** This paper presents CottonLeafVision, a deep learning framework that accurately classifies cotton leaf diseases with 98% accuracy using a DenseNet201 model and provides explainability and robustness techniques

**Key Innovation:** The use of Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping, occlusion sensitivity analysis, and adversarial training to enhance model reliability and interpretability

**Why It Matters:** The development of CottonLeafVision has significant practical implications for the textile industry and agriculture, enabling precise identification and detection of cotton leaf diseases for economic stability

**Tags:** deep learning, computer vision, cotton leaf disease classification, explainable AI, agricultural technology

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14686v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14686v1)

## 3. Expert-Driven Survival Machines: Improving Stratification and Interpretability in Multiple Clinical Cohorts

**Authors:** Farica Zhuang, Zixuan Wen, Christos Davatzikos, Li Shen

**TL;DR:** This paper proposes a novel deep learning framework that improves survival prediction in clinical cohorts by leveraging a mixture-of-experts approach to capture heterogeneous patient patterns

**Key Innovation:** The introduction of a routing-based expert mechanism that dynamically allocates patients to specialized risk predictors within a parametric survival modeling framework

**Why It Matters:** The proposed method has the potential to enable more accurate and personalized risk stratification, leading to improved patient management and outcomes in various disease domains

**Tags:** survival analysis, deep learning, mixture-of-experts, clinical cohorts, personalized medicine

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14608v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14608v1)
