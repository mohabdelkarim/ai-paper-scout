## 1. Tokenisation is not in APX

**Authors:** Violeta Kastreva, Philip Whittington, Dennis Komm, Tiago Pimentel

**TL;DR:** This paper proves that finding the optimal way to split text into tokens is computationally intractable, meaning no efficient algorithm can guarantee a near-perfect solution.

**Key Innovation:** A formal proof that tokenization is APX-hard, demonstrating that no polynomial-time algorithm can approximate the optimal tokenization within any constant factor under standard compression or likelihood objectives.

**Why It Matters:** Engineers can stop wasting compute resources on exhaustive search or complex optimization heuristics for perfect tokenization, instead focusing on fast, heuristic-based methods that are provably sufficient for practical LLM deployment.

**Tags:** computational-complexity, tokenization, approximation-algorithms, natural-language-processing, theoretical-cs

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34938v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34938v1)

## 2. Repairing the Trustworthiness of Distilled Language Models

**Authors:** Kumar Shubham, Devendra Vyas, Vidushi Agarwal, Jayin Khanna, Prathosh AP

**TL;DR:** This paper introduces a method to fix safety and trustworthiness issues in smaller AI models created by compressing larger ones, without needing the original training data or degrading the model's general performance.

**Key Innovation:** A targeted repair framework using the proximal Bregman response function (PBRF) with a surrogate proxy dataset to estimate curvature, combined with an odds-ratio objective for preference-based supervision that constrains changes to the distilled model.

**Why It Matters:** Enables engineers to deploy smaller, cost-effective language models in production environments by allowing them to patch specific safety regressions introduced during compression without the high cost and data requirements of re-distilling the model from scratch.

**Tags:** knowledge-distillation, ai-safety, model-repair, preference-optimization, llm-compression

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34831v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34831v1)

## 3. Collect for Coverage, Replay with Evidence: Adaptive and Efficient On-Policy Distillation for Long-Horizon Agents

**Authors:** Haowen Hou, Gen Li, Ruilin Li, Yicheng Liu, Zhen Huang, Shuai Dong, Congcong Wang, Kaiwen Tuo, Hongrui Wu, Zelin Peng, Kanghui Tian, Ji Zhao, Dianyi Wang, Jiaqi Wang

**TL;DR:** A new method called CARE-OPD makes training long-horizon AI agents significantly faster and more efficient by intelligently selecting which past experiences to reuse and which new data to collect, rather than relying solely on expensive real-time interactions.

**Key Innovation:** The introduction of CARE-OPD, which combines student-seeded teacher completions with confidence gating to expand history coverage, and hindsight replay allocation that uses downstream execution evidence to identify and prioritize critical decision points within long trajectories.

**Why It Matters:** Enables the practical deployment of complex AI agents for tasks like software engineering and deep research by reducing end-to-end training time by 45% and environment actions by 44%, thereby lowering computational costs and accelerating the iteration cycle for building autonomous systems.

**Tags:** on-policy-distillation, long-horizon-agents, experience-replay, reinforcement-learning, efficiency

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34756v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34756v1)
