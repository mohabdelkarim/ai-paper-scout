# AI Paper Scout Report — 2026-11-12

## Selection Rationale

## 1. AutoScaffold: AI-Guided Scaffolding for Reinforcement Learning of Language Models

**Authors:** Zijian Zhang, Rizhen Hu, Jiangweizhi Peng, Shiyang Li, Sujay Bhatt, Sihan Zeng, Mingyi Hong

**TL;DR:** AutoScaffold uses a frozen teacher AI to automatically generate and adapt training hints for a student AI, significantly improving its performance in reasoning and coding tasks without human intervention.

**Key Innovation:** A unified AI-for-AI framework where a frozen teacher analyzes a student model's training failures to dynamically design and adapt scaffolds (strategies and hints) that are removed at evaluation time.

**Why It Matters:** Enables engineers to train more capable language models for complex tasks like GPU kernel optimization and mathematical reasoning with fewer training steps and less manual prompt engineering, reducing the cost and time required to deploy specialized AI agents.

**Tags:** reinforcement-learning, ai-for-ai, scaffolding, language-models, automated-prompting

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14515v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14515v1)

## 2. A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets

**Authors:** Audrey Huang, Nan Jiang, Sham M. Kakade, Sadhika Malladi, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Dylan J. Foster

**TL;DR:** This paper provides a theoretical framework for language model post-training, identifying why standard methods like REINFORCE can fail to find optimal behaviors despite improving rewards, and proving that specific regularization techniques ensure stable and efficient convergence.

**Key Innovation:** The identification of 'coverage collapse' in standard policy optimization algorithms and the formalization of 'reference resets' within the Approximate Policy Mirror Descent (APMD) meta-algorithm to guarantee global convergence and preserve the base model's ability to generate optimal responses.

**Why It Matters:** Engineers can use these insights to configure PPO and GRPO hyperparameters—specifically KL regularization strength and reference policy updates—to prevent training stagnation, thereby reducing the computational cost and time required to fine-tune large language models effectively.

**Tags:** policy-optimization, reinforcement-learning, post-training, kl-regularization, convergence-guarantees

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467v1)

## 3. TimeSeek: Multi-Task and Multi-Domain Pretraining for Generalized Time Series Analytics

**Authors:** Peng Chen, Yihang Wang, Zhongwen Rao, Yang Shu, Yuying Qiu, Yuxuan Chen, Lujia Pan, Bin Yang, Chenjuan Guo

**TL;DR:** TimeSeek is a unified time series model that uses adaptive feature extraction and multi-task training to handle forecasting, anomaly detection, and classification across different domains with high accuracy and efficiency.

**Key Innovation:** The introduction of a Multi-Domain Feature Extractor for adaptive temporal and channel modeling combined with a multi-task collaborative training framework that balances task specificity and commonality.

**Why It Matters:** Enables engineers to deploy a single, versatile model for diverse operational tasks like predictive maintenance and fraud detection without needing separate specialized models for each task or domain, reducing infrastructure complexity and improving zero-shot performance in new environments.

**Tags:** time-series, foundation-models, multi-task-learning, pretraining, domain-adaptation

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14491v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14491v1)
